Description: "Christian joined the Cliffe Laboratory as an Intermediate Laboratory Specialist I to investigate the molecular mechanisms governing Herpes Simplex Virus 1 (HSV-1) latency and reactivation. His current research focuses on elucidating how the host transcription factor c-Fos regulates HSV-1 reactivation in primary neuronal cultures.

Prior to joining the University of Virginia, Christian earned a Bachelor of Science in Biology from the National University of Trujillo, Peru. In 2024, he completed a Master's degree in Virology at the Complutense University of Madrid, where he investigated the viral microbiome of wild birds using metagenomic approaches.

His research expertise encompasses primary cell culture, quantitative RT-PCR, Western blotting, viral particle isolation, and computational genomics. His work integrates molecular virology and bioinformatics to better understand host–virus interactions and the molecular networks that regulate chronic neuronal infections."
